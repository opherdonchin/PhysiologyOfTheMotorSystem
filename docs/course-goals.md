# Course goals — Physiology of the Motor System

**Status:** Instructor-approved Q2 goals, agreed in discussion on 2026-10-16–17 and recorded on 2026-10-17. This is the authoritative course-level statement of the agreed goals, not a prescription of teaching methods or a claim that student materials have been published.

## Agreed goals

### Physiological understanding

Students should develop a solid understanding of the organization, anatomical connections, and physiological functions of the major structures involved in motor control and learning, sufficient to read and understand contemporary research that investigates how these structures interact to produce behavior.

### Reading scientific research

Through guided reading and class discussion, students will learn how to approach scientific papers, identify research questions, follow the logic of experiments, and understand how findings relate to authors’ conclusions.

### Developing understanding through writing

Through writing, students will learn to move beyond summarizing scientific material to develop their own understanding of motor-system physiology and research, making connections, formulating questions, and developing and refining interpretations and ideas of their own.

## Agreed interpretation and boundaries

- The physiology goal is the central disciplinary purpose. Reading and writing serve that purpose while developing students' scientific literacy.
- Reading is **guided**. Students are not required to acquire independent-paper-reading mastery within this course.
- **Critical evaluation is practiced and modeled, not a separately assessed skill.** In class, following the paper's experimental logic can open a discussion of whether the evidence supports the authors' conclusions, alternative interpretations, or limitations. Students should gain experience with that reasoning, but critical evaluation is not an additional outcome requiring demonstrated proficiency or independent assessment.
- Writing is a way to think and develop understanding, not merely an exercise in summarizing material or correcting English prose.
- No formal week-by-week scientific-literacy skill progression, mastery milestones, or assessment ladder is required. Assignments and classroom demands can evolve pragmatically without becoming a formal competency sequence.
- These statements distinguish *course goals* from *methods*. The actual lecture and paper-discussion workflows (Q3), artifact-development skills (Q4), source policy (Q5), and detailed course architecture and planned/actual maps (Q7) remain to be designed or agreed. Existing syllabus obligations are not silently rewritten by this document.
